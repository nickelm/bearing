# Plan: World diversity (track W)

_Drafted 2026-11-17 for [proposal 0007](../proposals/0007-world-diversity.md) (decision 204). The tasks are in `TODO.md` under the world diversity track; later decisions record what each milestone changes. Where this plan and the proposal differ, the plan says why, and decision 204 lists the readings for the owner to confirm. The baseline is `docs/reports/world-diversity-baseline.md`._

## Goal

Worlds that differ from each other and from region to region within themselves: continents with a tectonic and climatic identity, landforms that follow rock and history, a biome set that covers Earth's memorable landscapes, closed basins with salt lakes and playas, named zones with their own look, and a handful of named wonders. The proposal keeps the current terrain and erosion as the backbone. This plan does the same: every new stage either feeds the existing stages new inputs (uplift amplitude, erodibility, landform masks, biome rules) or adds a layer beside them (zones, props, wonders), and none replaces a working stage.

## Where the proposal meets the code

Each item has a resolution that decision 204 records.

1. **Scale** (owner, decision 205). The radius in the code (`PLANET_RADIUS`, 1,000 km) equals the design doc's. The owner asked for the appropriate scaling, which is decision 121's split. Regional extents follow the landmasses and scale by R / R_Earth ≈ 0.157: zones of 100–400 km become 16–63 km, blends of 5–15 km become about 1–2.5 km, and the 100 km windows of the local-variety criterion become about 16 km. Landforms inside a province (fault-block spacing of 15–30 km, gullies, canyon widths, props) and all heights keep Earth's values. Counts per world (5–15 wonders) stay as written. A world then has a few thousand zones.
2. **Heights.** "Peaks up to 8–9 km" and "peaks above 6 km" are physical meters after the landform gain, the heights the HUD and the climate's Earth elevation use. Today every range shares one amplitude per boundary type and one soft limit (6,500 m, 40% above). _Resolution:_ provinces scale the uplift amplitude (reference meters) and carry their own soft limit and compression in `LANDFORM`: young collision ranges get a knee near 7,500 m so peaks reach 8–9 km; old mountains a low amplitude and a knee near 2,000 m so they top out near 2.5 km; volcanic cones and hotspot shields add their own profiles. The landform gain of 12 stays. The criterion is measured on the eroded global grid at 1024²; the tiles' alpine crests add a few hundred meters above it.
3. **Two tiers of facts.** Campaign facts come from the coarse pass on the CPU, because GPU erosion is not bit-identical across GPUs (decision 65), and campaign logic reads only the strategic graph (rule 9). Salt-flat airstrip sites, zone and wonder names in briefings, and provinces as barriers are campaign facts. _Resolution:_ provinces, lithology, continental character, the climate modifiers, the biome rules, closed basins, zones and wonder candidates are pure functions of the tectonic data and the cell direction, evaluated on the coarse pass (256²) for the graph and again on the global grid (1024²) for erosion and tiles. The graph reads only the coarse evaluation. What needs erosion (fjords, canyons, cirques, playa outlines at full resolution, prop placement) is execution and presentation data.
4. **No tectonic history.** Plates have no age, boundaries no age, and there are no hotspots. Adding fields to the `plates` stream would change every stage after it. _Resolution:_ a new `provinces` stage after `boundaries` and before `uplift`, drawing from `rng(seed, "provinces")` with forks `ages`, `sutures`, `hotspots`, `characters`. It gives each boundary run (a connected stretch of one boundary type) an age, young or old, weighted by closing speed; each continental plate a craton age; a few fossil sutures inside old continental plates (old mountains, as the Urals are); and 3–8 hotspots with tracks along their plate's motion. Plates, crust and boundaries stay byte-identical (a test).
5. **Order of provinces, climate and continents.** Glaciated lowland, karst and soft sediment depend on climate or rock, but uplift must know the province before climate runs; continental character biases provinces, but landmasses are labeled from heights that come after uplift. _Resolution:_ two passes and a crust-based continent. Tectonic provinces (the first nine rows of the proposal's table) are assigned before uplift. Continents for character are the continental crust of each plate (decision 210: crust is welded into one region in most seeds), known after the crust stage. Karst platform and soft sediment are lithology classes of the sedimentary basin province, decided in the `lithology` stage after elevation; glaciated lowland is a surface province written after climate, from the existing glaciation reach, over shields and basins only. One province map holds all twelve.
6. **Steering.** Rule 10 forbids steering generation toward a particular geography; the only rejection is the validity check. Character biases provinces, and the distinctness check nudges parameters. _Resolution:_ the character of each continent is drawn from what its tectonics allow (a continent without a collision boundary cannot be "young mountains"), weighted by its climate belt, from `provinces/characters`. The distinctness check runs on the coarse pass after biomes: if the two largest continents' joint province and biome distributions fall under the divergence threshold, the smaller one's character is redrawn from `provinces/characters/nudge-k` (k = 1, 2), excluding the larger one's, and the stages from `provinces` to `biomes` run again. It never rejects a world and never favors any character or theater. Rule 10 is amended to allow exactly this (`CLAUDE.md`). The redraws are stored with the world, so the global pass never re-decides.
7. **Erodibility.** `kdt` is a WGSL constant. _Resolution:_ an erodibility map (a factor of about 0.3 for granite and basalt to 3 for loess and ash) uploaded with the global grid and multiplied into the incision; the talus angle per lithology in the thermal pass. Defaults keep today's constant where the factor is 1, so a test can still pin the uniform case.
8. **Terracing everywhere dry.** The aridity mask gates strata and buttes, so every desert, most shrubland and a fifth of the grassland is layered: a median of 33% of all land over the baseline's 20 seeds. _Resolution:_ the reserved channel y of the masks' second word becomes a **layered rock** mask from lithology (horizontal sandstone, shale and limestone), and strata and buttes need arid × layered. Rocky and sand deserts lose their benches; red rock deserts keep them.
9. **Closed basins.** Drainage's priority flood starts from the ocean, so every depression spills to the sea. _Resolution:_ a water balance on the filled depressions in `computeDrainage`, on both grids. For each depression (a fill region with its spill cell), the inflow is the runoff of its catchment and the loss is lake evaporation (the climate's potential evapotranspiration over the lake's area at each level, from a hypsometric table of the depression). The lake rises to the level where loss meets inflow. Below the spill level the basin is closed: its receivers end at the lake, nothing leaves it, and its lake is salt (salinity from the ratio of loss to inflow). With no balance above the floor it is a playa. The fill stays ε-sloped, so river profiles stay monotone and end at the lake surface; `riverInvariants` gains a check that endorheic rivers end at their lake.
10. **The salt pans that exist.** The tiles already flatten lake beds in dry land into white pans (decision 130), from the lake depth and the aridity, without a water balance. _Resolution:_ the kernel draws playas and salt lakes from the basin records (lake level, balance level, salinity) and adds pale shoreline rings between the balance level and the highest former shoreline; decision 130's arid lake rule becomes the case "playa".
11. **Salt flats in the graph.** _Resolution:_ playas larger than about 25 km² on the coarse pass become a feature kind `SaltFlat` and airstrip sites in `strategic/sites.ts` (a site kind `dry-lake`, exempt from the lake exclusion). Casting may use them later (emergency and secret bases are a C-track task); W3 only records them.
12. **Biome table.** Seventeen values; the proposal's twenty land biomes plus the four water values fit under the tile covers at 32. Many consumers key on the old values. _Resolution:_ `Biome` v2 with stable water values (0–3) and the twenty land biomes from 4; each consumer table is rewritten from the proposal (palette, vegetation weights, signature); a mapping from old to new for the tests that pin old behavior; `GENERATOR_VERSION` up. Shrubland disappears: it becomes Mediterranean scrub where summers are dry, cold desert and steppe where cold, savanna where hot. Alpine becomes alpine meadow below the snow line, with rock and ice above drawn by the shader as now. The tile covers (beach, marsh, gravel) stay; the global wetland and mangrove biomes are the large-area versions of marsh and the tiles' mangroves.
13. **Seasonality.** Mediterranean scrub and monsoon forest need precipitation seasons; the climate has an annual total only. _Resolution:_ a precipitation seasonality field (−1 winter-wet to +1 summer-wet) from the seasonal shift of the wind cells with the tilt and from west-coast exposure, in `climate/modifiers`, with the cold currents (west coasts between about 15° and 35° latitude: cooler, drier, foggier) and continentality (larger range and drier air with distance from the windward coast). The weather model reads the fog tendency.
14. **Aspect and ecotones at the right scale.** The global grid's 1.5 km cells resolve only large slopes. _Resolution:_ aspect enters where the faces are: the shader's shade choice and the canopy density in `canopy.ts`, which the renderer and the sim share (decision 148), so a north-slope forest is an obstacle where it is drawn. Ecotone noise follows the same split: on the global grid for biome boundaries, in the shader and canopy for fine interfingering.
15. **Zones and local variety.** A zone is 16–63 km of similar province and biome, while the local-variety criterion asks for entropy over 16 km windows on 90% of land; a coherent zone has low entropy by design. _Resolution:_ the entropy is measured over the joint classes the player sees at 1.5 km (biome with its altitude zone, province, and surface character: riparian, ecotone, disturbance, lake), on the eroded global grid, where a 16 km window holds about 90 cells. A zone keeps its identity while its valleys, slopes and rivers vary. The minimum starts at 0.5 bit on 90% of land and is set from the eroded baseline (the coarse baseline over unscaled 100 km windows meets 0.5 bit on a median of 70% of land; the scaled windows give less).
16. **Zones in the code.** At 16–63 km a zone is three to ten coarse cells across, too coarse to segment or to follow a ridge. _Resolution:_ zones are presentation data, like the alignment tier: segmented once per world on the eroded global grid (1.5 km cells, 11–42 cells across) in the worker's finish, by region growing on province and biome group, merged and split to 16–63 km, with boundaries pulled to barriers (ridges, rivers, coasts, escarpments), and stored with the world. Each zone gets a look recipe from `rng(seed, "zones")` within fixed limits and a name from `names/zones`, both in zone order, so they are the same on one machine. A distance to the nearest boundary per cell gives the 1–2.5 km blends where no barrier is. Campaign logic never reads zones (rule 9); briefings name wonders, not zones, and the weather's tendencies (low cloud, fog, wind) come from biome and province, which exist on the coarse pass. In the simulation view a zone texture gives each fragment its palette shift; haze, sky grading, sun warmth, cloud regime and ambient particles blend by the camera's position. On the map, zone labels are area labels from the stored records; they replace the labels of the deserts, forests, plains, plateaus and wetlands they cover, at the Theater band for zones over about 40 km and at the Region band for all, while ranges, peaks, hills and rivers keep theirs.
17. **Props and the one height function.** Rule 13 sends every height query through one function of terrain edits; props are not terrain, and their sides overhang. _Resolution:_ props are obstacles like the canopy. A pure placement function in `worldgen/props.ts` (by cell, from province, lithology and the tile's relief, `rng(seed, "props")` forked per cell) is shared by the renderer and the sim; each prop has a simple collision shape (capsule, prism or box) that the flight model's contact (`sim/flight/contact.ts`), the camera's clearance and later line of sight query beside the canopy. The height function stays the ground's. Drowned towers stand at sea on the shelf.
18. **Wonders and the cast.** The proposal favors wonders "where they will be seen (near fronts, sea lanes, and airbases)", which the cast decides, while their terrain must exist in the tiles. _Resolution:_ the graph builder records wonder candidates from detectors over the coarse world (conditions per kind); a campaign step between casting's territory and its briefing selects 5–15 of them, weighted toward fronts, lanes and airbases, from `rng(seed, "wonders")`, so casting's own stream is unchanged; names come from `names/wonders`. Shaping is the tile kernel's (tepui walls, a caldera, travertine steps, a knickpoint for a falls) or props (karst bays, pillar forests, basalt columns), from the selected list. The router keeps alignments out of a wonder's footprint through its extra cost term (decision 180) and lets a scenic route pass within sight.
19. **A brighter palette.** Part I avoids saturated primaries; Part II reserves saturation for the military overlays. _Resolution:_ Part I takes the proposal's palette as starting values, none of which is a primary; brine, travertine pools and coral lagoons are the most saturated surfaces and occur in small areas. Part II derives its fills by mixing the middle shade toward paper (`#F4EFE4`, about 35%) and capping saturation below the faction colors, so the territory tint and badges keep their lead; acceptance checks 3 and 4 decide. The post-processing grade is retuned in the render preview once the palette lands.
20. **Gullies finer than the grid.** The kernel has gullies (decision 126); micro-facets are off by default (decision 197). _Resolution:_ the kernel's gullies take a soft-sediment density from the province mask, and the finer gullies are shade lines in the terrain shader, aligned to the face's downslope direction and fading in below about 2 km, independent of the micro-facets.
21. **Determinism.** _Resolution:_ new streams `provinces`, `lithology`, `climate/modifiers` (a fork of a new top-level `climate-modifiers` stream, so `climate`'s own forks are untouched), `zones`, `props`, `wonders`, and the forks `names/zones` and `names/wonders`. Plates, crust and boundaries stay byte-identical; every stage from uplift on changes by design, so every seed's world and cast change (`GENERATOR_VERSION` up; the owner accepted changing seeds in decision 163).
22. **Generation time.** The criterion allows 50% more. The new CPU stages are cheap except the distinctness rerun, which repeats provinces to biomes (about 3.5 s of the coarse pass's climate and uplift) up to twice. _Resolution:_ at most two redraws; the climate grid is reused between them where only provinces change; times per stage are reported by the metrics tool.
23. **Branch.** The owner's brief for the unattended session asks for a branch `feature/world-diversity` and a pull request; `CLAUDE.md` says `main` (decision 163). _Resolution:_ the brief is the owner's later and specific instruction for that session, whose work they want to review before it reaches the deployed site, so that session follows it; every other session follows `CLAUDE.md`. This plan was committed to `main`.

## Architecture

### Stages

```
coarse pass (CPU, 256²) and global pass (GPU 1024², same functions)
  plates ─► crust ─► boundaries ─► PROVINCES (ages, sutures, hotspots, continental characters)
        ─► uplift (amplitude and profile by province) ─► elevation
        ─► LITHOLOGY (rock by province, noise and layering; karst and soft sediment)
        ─► climate + CLIMATE MODIFIERS (seasonality, cold currents, continentality, orography)
        ─► biomes v2 (zonation, soil from lithology, ecotones) ─► glaciated lowland (province overlay)
        ─► distinctness check (≤ 2 redraws of provinces → biomes)
  coarse:  drainage with CLOSED BASINS ─► graph (salt flats, airstrip sites, WONDER candidates)
           ─► casting ─► WONDER selection ─► briefing
  global:  detail ─► erosion (erodibility, talus by rock; glacial carving with cirques, arêtes, fjords)
           ─► drainage with closed basins ─► rivers ─► biomes v2 ─► ZONES (segmented, named, stored)
tiles:     landform masks (+ layered rock, province classes) ─► basin and range blocks, canyons,
           restricted strata and buttes, playas and shorelines, wonder shaping ─► rivers last
render:    zone texture (palette shift), zone uniforms (haze, grade, sun, clouds, particles),
           props (instanced, LOD), strategic fills, patterns, zone labels, wonder glyphs
sim:       props as obstacles beside the canopy; zone weather tendencies
```

## Verification

## Risks

- **Every cast changes.** Uplift, climate and biomes feed settlements, roads, the front and the cast. The theater mix over 20 seeds (`npm run seeds`) is compared before and after W2 and W4; a large shift in front or lane frequency is reported to the owner, not tuned away (rule 10).
- **Erodibility contrasts make spikes.** Hard rock beside soft rock can leave knife ridges at 1.5 km cells. Smooth the factor over a few cells and cap the contrast at 10.
- **Taller ranges against the 20 km envelope and the terrain's horizon.** Peaks of 9 km shorten the vertical room under the simulation view's ceiling and lengthen the horizon distance for tall terrain; measure the tile budget at `ceiling` and `range` views after W2.
- **Closed basins swallow rivers.** A misjudged balance turns a major river into a lake in the desert. Compare the river network's length and the largest rivers' discharge to the baseline; arid closed basins should take a small share of the land's runoff.
- **Too many zones or too few.** Region growing on noisy classes fragments. The size criterion and a merge pass for zones under 16 km keep it bounded.
- **Brighter palette against the look.** The reference board's mood comes from warm light on muted ground. Render checks 1–5 after W4 and W6 and compare with the board; the grade may need to pull back.
